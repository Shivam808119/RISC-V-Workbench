The core, explained

BOOM, the Berkeley Out-of-Order Machine, is built for speed. Instead of waiting for each instruction, it renames registers, queues work and runs whatever is ready, then retires everything in order.

BOOM renames registers, queues instructions, and guesses branches to stay busy. It reuses Rocket’s caches and MMU. The guessing is what opens the door to Spectre.

To do this it guesses branches and loads data early. Most of the memory system, including the caches and the MMU, is borrowed from Rocket, so the new part is the engine that runs ahead.

2

Memory it uses, and why

Memory is where speed and security meet. Here is every memory part in BOOM v2, with the reason it is there.

Register file and ROBA physical register file, a free list, and the reorder buffer.
Why it is here: Renaming removes false dependencies, and the ROB lets instructions finish out of order but retire in order.
Load-store queuesLAQ, SAQ and SDQ track loads and stores, so loads can run early.
Why it is here: Separate queues let loads run early while stores wait for their data, and still keep the right order.
Caches from RocketL1 instruction cache and the non-blocking HellaCache.
Why it is here: Reusing Rocket’s caches saved design time and gave BOOM a proven base.
MMUITLB, DTLB, page walker and PMP, reused from Rocket.
Why it is here: The same translation and protection hardware as Rocket gives BOOM full operating-system support.
Shared L2TileLink coherence and RVWMO ordering.
Why it is here: The L2 and coherence let multiple BOOM tiles share data.
Follow one load. It is renamed, placed in the load queue and issued as soon as its address is ready, even before older branches are resolved. It reads the L1 data cache, and the line is brought in. If the branch was wrong, the instruction is squashed, but the cache line it fetched stays.
3

Security it uses, and why

That left-behind line is the Spectre channel. BOOM keeps Meltdown out by checking the TLB and PMP before data is used, but speculation, shared predictors and shared caches are all open until software or hardware mitigations are added.
